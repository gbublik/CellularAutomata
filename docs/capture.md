# Slice capture — `Automata/Capture/`

PNG slices rasterised straight out of the grid, series capture, tiling and capture presets. Read before touching F6/F7 or the slice rasteriser.

- **`Automata/Capture/`** — export an orthogonal slice as a PNG, for use as a texture: masks, ornaments, overlays. **F6** writes to `Saved/AutomataSlices/`, **Shift+F6** opens a save dialog.

**This is not a screenshot, and that is the whole point.** The image is rasterised straight out of `FCellGrid`; no GPU, no `SceneCapture`, no tiled assembly. Under a strictly axis-aligned orthogonal projection a cell lands exactly on a cell of a 2D grid, so: there is no antialiasing *by construction* (a pixel is a cell — intermediate values have nowhere to come from), the size is bounded by neither the window nor the 16384 render-target limit, and the result is reproducible regardless of where the camera sits or how it is zoomed. Measured against the screenshot it replaces: **120 KB at 512² versus 1.5 MB**, and sharper — few distinct colours compress well precisely because nothing is smoothed. What is lost is bloom, shadows and highlights: the image is flat, the cell's own ramp colour. For a mask that is the point; it will not look like a beauty shot.

**The camera only chooses a side.** Its own basis is snapped to the nearest world axes, and what lands in frame is decided by the active filters — cull volume, age filter, view slice — not by the screen rectangle. Cut with the box and the slice contains exactly the box's contents. That falls out for free because the cells come from `BuildCellRenderData()`, the very same collection that feeds the on-screen render: there is nowhere for a second set of "what to show" rules to appear, let alone drift. (The plan called for factoring that collection into a shared helper; it wasn't needed — `BuildCellRenderData()` already returns `{world position, colour}` with every filter applied, which is exactly the rasteriser's input, and refactoring the hot render path for it would have been risk without gain.)

**Orientation is where this goes wrong, so it is worth stating.** `BuildAxes()` snaps forward and up, but **derives the horizontal as a cross product** rather than snapping it too — snapping all three independently lets a diagonal view (NumPad7 isometric) collapse two of them onto the same axis, and lets rounding noise produce a mirrored image, which is invisible on a symmetric pattern. The axis of sight is also excluded from the "up" candidates for the same reason. `Slice.Orientation` guards this with a deliberately **asymmetric** figure (a "Г"), because a symmetric one passes while transposed *and* while mirrored.

**Gamma is the trap that leaves no trace.** `BuildAgeColorLut()` produces *linear* bytes — that is what the material needs (`PerInstanceCustomData` is a raw float, see `FCellRenderInstance`) — but every image viewer reads a PNG as sRGB, so writing those bytes straight out yields a picture visibly darker than the screen, with nothing in the log. Both LUT builders therefore take a `bSRGB` parameter (default off, so the render path is unchanged) and the capture passes `true`, controlled by `FSliceCaptureParams::bEncodeSRGB`. Turn it off only when the slice goes back into the engine as a mask with sRGB disabled on the texture.

**Series capture (F7, again to abort)** turns this from "a screenshot tool" into a way to watch an evolution frame by frame: frames land in their own `Series_<date>/` subfolder as `Frame_0000.png`… ready to be assembled into an animation or spread out as variants of one ornament. The step is counted in **generations**, not screen frames or seconds — the pattern changes by generation, and only that makes the series evenly spaced regardless of simulation speed or of how many generations one background dispatch happened to compute. The first frame is the current state, the one the user was looking at when they decided to record.

It rides the ordinary simulation rather than spinning its own loop: a loop would freeze the editor for minutes on a large grid, whereas this shows progress and can be interrupted. If Play was stopped, the series starts it and stops it again at the end; if Play was already running, it leaves it alone — the series is an observer, not an owner.

**`bSeriesFastMode` (default on) skips the on-screen render entirely while recording** — the capture rasterises from the grid, so a generation does not need to reach the screen to reach a file, and `AddInstances` is ~97% of a frame's cost. The inter-step delay is dropped too: `Speed` paces *viewing*, and identical files would just take proportionally longer to produce. The picture therefore sits still during a series (progress goes to the status line) — **and stays on the state F7 was pressed from after it ends too: `StopSeriesCapture()` deliberately does no catch-up render.** The final generation is already in the last PNG, and rendering a multi-million-cell grid is the single most expensive operation there is, so paying for it to show a frame that is being looked at in the files anyway is pure loss. The screen refreshes on the next render of its own accord (Space, F, R, N). **The ordering in `ApplyStepResult()` is load-bearing**: the last frame's `CaptureSeriesFrame()` ends the series by calling `StopSeriesCapture()`, which clears `bSeriesCaptureActive` — so the skip-render decision is snapshotted into a local (`bSeriesSkipsRender`) *before* the capture block rather than read at the `return` below it. Read it after, and the final generation — alone out of all of them — still reaches `AddInstances`.

**Tiling (`ESliceTileMode`) is mirror-based, and that is a deliberate ceiling.** Reflection seams up unconditionally — pixels along the mirror line coincide by construction — so any slice becomes a seamless tile, at the cost of the result always being symmetric. A pattern that tiles *without* symmetry would require the automaton to be computed on a torus, which is a property of the simulation rather than of the capture, and would touch `FCellGrid`, both compute strategies and the `.usf` shader; that was considered and deliberately not done.

The one thing worth doing in code rather than by hand in an editor: **the edge row is not duplicated**. The tile is `[0..W-1][W-2..1]`, width `2W-2` — not `[0..W-1][W-1..0]`. Duplicating the edge puts a double line on every seam, exactly the artifact the whole per-pixel approach exists to avoid. It also leaves the tile closed on itself: the column after the last one is column 0 of the next copy, and that pair is as symmetric as any pair inside. `Slice.Tile` pins this down with a four-colour strip (on a uniform image a wrong mirror looks right) and checks that a one-pixel axis is left alone rather than collapsing to `2*1-2 == 0`.

Note the budget is checked on the image *before* mirroring, so a tile can come out nearly four times `MaxCapturePixels` in area.

Four depth modes (`ECellRasterMode`): `NearestToCamera` (what the screen shows), `Silhouette` (two-colour stencil — the cheapest mode and the most direct answer to "I need a mask"), `Thickness` (cells along the ray, normalised to greys — a thickness map that is not visible on screen at all). Every mode must be **order-independent**: `GetAliveCells()` iterates a `TMap` and its order changes between generations, so a mode that depended on it would produce different pixels for the same state. `PixelsPerCell` scales by block copy, never interpolation. `MaxCapturePixels` (8192², about half the peak — pixel buffer plus PNG buffer) is checked *before* any allocation, the `MaxGeneratedCells` idiom.

**`CapturePresets` (`Automata/Capture/CapturePresets.h/.cpp`, plain namespace + `FCapturePreset` `USTRUCT(BlueprintType)`) — whole capture setups, cycled with Shift+F7**, following `RulePresets`/`RenderPresets` exactly (code-constant table, `BlueprintReadOnly` fields, `namespace CapturePresets { const TArray<FCapturePreset>& GetAll(); }`, plus `GetCapturePresets()`/`ApplyCapturePreset(int32)`/`CycleCapturePreset()` on the orchestrator). Shift+F7 is to F7 what Shift+Y is to Y — the modifier picks *what to shoot with*, the bare key shoots — and it is checked inside `OnToggleSeriesCapture()` rather than mapped, the usual Enhanced-Input-can't-require-a-modifier idiom.

A preset stores a whole `FSliceCaptureParams` and is applied by assigning the struct, so switching presets can never leave a tail from the previous one — the same determinism argument as `FRenderPreset`, and the reason there is no "leave this field alone". Index 0 (`Обычная съёмка`) is default-constructed rather than hand-written, so it cannot drift from `FSliceCaptureParams`'s own defaults. `ApplyCapturePreset()` does not touch a running series: its length and step are fixed at start, and changing the frame size mid-series would produce a series of mismatched frames.

The other three are the **logo hunt**, and they are three different questions rather than three tastes: `Лого: силуэт` (does the *shape* read at all — `Silhouette`, 1 px/cell, 60 frames × 10 generations), `Лого: орнамент` (the same mirrored into a symmetric mark), `Лого: финал` (the winner in full colour at 8 px/cell on a transparent background, one frame). **Colour is deliberately absent from the hunt**: the mark is the cell arrangement, and the ramp is a swappable test palette — a knowingly late, free decision, since the capture rasterises from the grid, so the same seed and the same generation re-shoot bit-identically under any `AgeColors`. The series step is coarse because adjacent generations are near-identical: a fine step yields the same candidates several times over, not more of them.

**Every series folder gets a passport** — `WriteSeriesManifest()`, called from `StartSeriesCapture()` before the first frame (so even a series aborted immediately leaves a folder that explains itself). `Series.casave` goes through `WriteStateToFile()`, the exact Ctrl+S path down to the thumbnail — a near-identical "almost a save" format would drift from the real one at the first header change — and `Series.txt` says the same in words plus how to re-shoot a given frame. **A PNG carries no rule, seed or generation**, so a candidate carried off into another folder cannot be re-shot larger, recoloured or mirrored a month later, which is precisely what is wanted once one is chosen; frame `N` is generation `StartGeneration + N × SeriesGenerationsPerFrame`, and that offset is the one coordinate the image itself cannot hold. `WriteStateToFile()` therefore takes `bUpdateLastSavePath` — the passport must not become the target of the next silent Ctrl+S, or saving state would quietly relocate into a slices folder. Failure is warn-and-continue throughout: frames matter more than the passport. Note `FFileHelper::EEncodingOptions::ForceUTF8` already means *with* BOM (`ForceUTF8WithoutBOM` is the other one), and the BOM is required or Notepad renders the Cyrillic as garbage.
