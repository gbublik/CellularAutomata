# Initial-state generation — `Automata/Generation/`

Geometric generators, the neighbour-count analysis, the FCC/BCC parity filters, and the overload guard. Read before adding a generator or a lattice.

- **`Automata/Generation/`** — geometric generators of the *initial* state: lattices, solids, noise, symmetric seeds. Built for one specific effect the random-ball spawn cannot produce in principle — a large **metastable** structure that stands still under the current rule until one cell is knocked out of it, at which point a chain reaction spreads from that point. (The "knock a cell out" half needed no new code: Tab → click picks a single cell via `CellSelection::PickCellAlongRay()`, Delete kills it in place without resetting, R restores the structure for the next attempt.)

**Generators are deliberately *pure geometry*** — they never read `BirthCounts`/`SurvivalCounts` and never run trial steps. Whether a given structure is stable under a given rule is a question about the rule, and matching the two stays manual. What makes that matching arithmetic rather than guesswork is `FStateGeneratorParams::bAnalyzeNeighborCounts` (on by default): after generating, `StateGenerators::AnalyzeNeighborCounts()` logs a histogram of live-neighbor counts, **separately for live cells and for the empty cells adjacent to them** — still a property of the point set, not of the rule.

**`AAutomataOrchestrator::AnalyzeLiveStructure()` (Ctrl+Y) is the same measurement taken mid-run**, on `Grid`'s live cells instead of a generator's output — because the original only ever fires from `GenerateState()` (Y), so it describes the *generated* lattice and says nothing about what that lattice evolved into. It answers the question the generator version cannot: where the rule's thresholds fall relative to the distribution that actually exists right now. The histogram itself only says *what the distribution is*, so a **`ИТОГО` line follows it**, evaluated against the live `Survival`/`Birth` sets — how many cells are doomed, how many will be born, the net, and the resulting growth factor. That line is the actual deliverable; seventeen columns of `count:cells` do not answer "is this about to boom or collapse" at a glance, and one line does. Its growth factor is deliberately labelled an estimate: births are counted over empties *adjacent* to the sample, some of which lie outside the sub-cube, while the denominator is strictly the sample. Under Generations the same line switches wording from "умрут" to "уйдут в угасание" and appends a warning that births are overstated — decaying cells are not alive, so the histogram files them under "adjacent empty", yet birth-immunity means nothing can be born there. Under `0-6/1,3/2/ECFA`, for instance, survival ends at 6 of 26 neighbours, so the share of cells already past 7 is the share about to die — which is exactly the overcrowding collapse those rules cycle through. It needs no `bStepInProgress` guard: a background step only *reads* `*Grid`, this only reads, and the sole writer (`ApplyStepResult()`) is the same game thread that runs this.

The sample is a central sub-cube, `LiveAnalysisSampleExtent` (default 24), and that bound is **load-bearing for time, not memory**: cost grows as sample × offsets², since every sampled cell walks its neighbours and every empty neighbour then walks its own. At 24 that is ~117k cells and about a second; at 48 it is nearly a million and over ten. The `Occupied` set is built over *all* live cells either way, so the extent buys nothing on memory. Since an evolved structure is neither periodic nor necessarily centred, the sub-cube describes its **core rather than its growth front**, and the two genuinely differ — so the log always prints how many cells landed in the sample and what share of the total that is, rather than leaving the coverage implied. Measured (and locked down by `Generation.LatticeNeighborUniformity`): `LatticePlanes` with `Thickness=1` on a single axis gives **every** live cell exactly 8 Moore neighbours and **every** adjacent empty cell exactly 9 — so a rule with `8 ∈ S` and `9 ∉ B` holds it forever, and one missing cell drops its eight neighbours to 7 while raising the two empties above and below the hole to 8. `LatticeBlocks` 2×2×2 is the other good candidate (7 for live, 1/2/4 for empty — a much wider margin); `LatticeCheckerboard` is degenerate (12 both ways, and 0 under von Neumann), and `LatticeFrame` is the only lattice with a *non*-uniform count, which is exactly why it's worth measuring before use.

**`ArrayCells()` (Ctrl+D) is the Array modifier from Blender, over cells** — `Automata/Generation/CellArrayModifier.h`, parameters in `FCellArrayParams`. It is filed here but it is deliberately **not** an `EStateGeneratorType`, and the reason is the rule stated two paragraphs down: a generator builds a set out of nothing and never reads the scene, while this one works *over* an existing set. The only thing they share is the output — an array of cells handed to `RebuildGridFromCells()`.

The source is **the selection if there is one, otherwise every live cell**, with no switch between the two: a selection already means "work with this" and its absence means "work with everything", exactly as in a DCC package. Only cells that are still alive are taken from the selection (it survives simulation steps, and a cell under it may be long dead — the same filtering `StartFromSelection()` does). The result **replaces** the grid and becomes the new initial pattern: generation counter to zero, R returns to the array, Ctrl+S saves it, and every copy is age 0 including the one standing where the original was. In other words it is Enter with multiplication on the way, which is why it reuses `RebuildGridFromCells()` rather than the edit journal.

Unlike Blender, where one modifier multiplies along one axis and a 3D lattice of copies takes three of them stacked, `Count` names all three axes at once — there is no modifier stack here to layer arrays on. Copy `(i,j,k)` is the source shifted by `(i·stepX, j·stepY, k·stepZ)`; the array is strictly orthogonal, with no sheared variant. The step per axis is `round(sourceSize · RelativeOffset) + ConstantOffset` — the same two addends as Blender's relative and constant offsets, added the same way, so `RelativeOffset = 1` puts copies **flush**. That flushness is where the one real trap lives: the source's size is `Max − Min + 1`, cells not distance, and dropping the `+1` makes every copy overlap the previous one by exactly one cell along each axis — the most invisible failure this module has, which is why `Generation.ArrayModifier` asserts the six-cell contiguous run rather than the cell count.

Two deliberate departures. `bCenterOnSource` (on by default) lays the copies symmetrically around the source instead of growing them in the plus direction as Blender does — the camera is looking straight at the source when Ctrl+D is pressed, and a one-sided array walks out of frame the larger `Count` gets; at an even `Count` there is no exact centre and the shift rounds down. And **duplicates are not removed**: with overlapping copies a `TSet` over millions of cells would cost hundreds of megabytes and a whole pass, while `SetAlive()` is idempotent and `RebuildGridFromCells()` reads `InitialStateCells` back out of the grid where they have already collapsed — the same reasoning that lets `RandomBall` throw two darts into one cell. The budget is checked against `MaxGeneratedCells` before the grid is touched, using `SourceCount · Count.X · Count.Y · Count.Z` — an *upper* bound, computed in `int64` because that product overflows `int32` long before it reaches the ceiling. Two things are warned about rather than corrected, since neither is an error: a zero step on an axis being multiplied (all copies land on each other, so the result equals the source and the hotkey looks broken), and an **odd** step while a parity filter is active (half the copies land on the neighbouring sublattice — see `ECellParityFilter`, and the same concern as the even offset in `docs/persistence.md`).

**Generators return `TArray<FIntVector>`, they never touch `FCellGrid`.** That's load-bearing: `SetAlive()` caches the last chunk and is not thread-safe, so filling must stay sequential and in one place (`RebuildGridFromCells()`), while the generators themselves are free to parallelize, the cell count can be estimated up front, and the whole module is unit-testable without the engine (the header doesn't even forward-declare the grid). Files follow the established conventions exactly: `EStateGeneratorType`/`ESeedSymmetry` in their own headers (per `ENeighborhood`/`ESelectionCombineMode`), `StateGeneratorPresets.h/.cpp` mirroring `RulePresets`, and `StateGenerators.h/.cpp` as a flat namespace like `CellAging`/`CellMeshBuilder`.

`EStateGeneratorType` is **flat**, not "family + subtype": Shift+Y is then exactly `(uint8 + 1) % Num` (the `CycleChunkedRenderOrder()` idiom), `EditCondition` needs one comparison instead of two, and the HUD gets one dropdown. Family survives only as `FStateGeneratorPreset::FamilyName` — data for grouping, not a type. Likewise **all parameters live in one `FStateGeneratorParams`**, not four per-family structs, and the decisive reason is technical: `EditCondition` inside a USTRUCT resolves in *that struct's* scope, so `Type` must live inside it, and once it does, separate structs would each need their own copy of `Type`. `EditConditionHides` is mandatory rather than decorative — there are ~15 fields and only 3–5 are relevant at once.

**`GenerateRandom()` is gone, and with it the whole `Automata|Random` block (`Amount`, `SpawnRadius`, `ClusterFactor`).** The random ball stopped being a separate generation path and became what it always was underneath — `EStateGeneratorType::RandomBall`, with its own `Radius`/`Amount` inside `GenerationParams`. Every entry point now goes through `GenerateState()`: startup (`BeginPlay()`), **N** (`NewSeed()`, which re-rolls `Seed` and rebuilds — "same shape, different seed"), `Play` on an empty scene, and `ResetToInitialState()`'s defensive fallback. Choosing `RandomBall` reproduces the old behaviour exactly, which is why no switch was added: a second knob could only ever disagree with the generator selection.

Two consequences worth knowing. **There is no fallback at startup any more**: `GenerateState()` can legitimately *refuse* (the `MaxGeneratedCells` budget, a generator error) and by design leaves the grid untouched, so a bad configuration yields an empty world plus an on-screen message — deliberately, since drawing something *else* would leave the user guessing why the picture doesn't match the panel. And `ClusterFactor` turned out to be **already dead** before removal: `GenerateRandom()` only ever passed `Radius` and `Amount` into the generator, so the field had been steering nothing since generators arrived, merely being saved and printed.

**Editing a parameter builds the state on the spot** (`bAutoGenerateOnParamChange`, on by default) — generators are tuned by eye, in a loop of turn-a-knob-and-look that repeats dozens of times in a row, and a separate **Y** between the two halves of that loop is a beat of pure ceremony. It hangs off `PostEditChangeProperty()` and is therefore editor-only by construction; **Y** still works everywhere, including in PIE where the Details panel isn't at hand, and with the flag off.

Two details carry the feature. It rebuilds only on `ValueSet`, **never on `EPropertyChangeType::Interactive`** — that one arrives on every frame of a slider drag, and since generation wipes the grid and re-uploads the instances, dragging a radius across its range would mean a hundred full rebuilds and a wedged editor; waiting for the mouse to come up costs exactly one. And the trigger is matched on the event's **member** property rather than the changed property's name: `GenerationParams` is a struct, so a field edit reports `Radius`/`Amount`/`Type`, and asking for the outer member covers every field at once, including fields not yet added. `Seed` rides the same check (a plain property reports itself as its own member) because it belongs to the same formula and the same tuning loop; `MaxGeneratedCells` deliberately does **not** — it is the safety catch, and raising it means "permit this", not "build it now", so rebuilding on it would launch precisely the generation the catch had just refused. Since the path goes through the public `GenerateState()`, the budget estimate, the background-step refusal and the `InitialStateCells` return point are all shared with Y rather than re-implemented; and because `Seed` is not re-rolled, changing one field is an honest "same thing, bigger radius" comparison rather than a fresh random figure.

`Seed` survives, moved to `Automata|Generation` — every generator reads it, not just the ball. `RulePresets` now write their spawn settings into `GenerationParams.Radius`/`Amount` and set `Type = RandomBall` explicitly, since the catalogue's numbers describe a random ball of a given density and silently applying that radius to, say, a lattice of plates would change what the number means. Its random loop was copied *verbatim* (same order of `FRandRange` calls, same reject-sampling, same rounding) so a given `Seed` still produces the picture it did before; `Generation.RandomBallParity` is the golden test guarding exactly that, comparing against the pre-existing `AutomataTestUtils::SeedSphere()`.

**`bAutoReseedOnExtinction` (Shift+N) — re-roll the seed the moment the grid dies, and keep going.** A brute-force search for a seed that survives the rule: start the run, walk away, and the automaton keeps trying patterns until one of them lives. It exists because extinction is what *usually* happens to a random pattern, and finding a live one by hand was a loop of "Space, watch, everything goes dark, N, Space" — the mode is that loop with the human taken out. `AutoReseedCount` counts the attempts (zeroed on every switch-on), and each death is logged with how many generations the seed managed, which is the only thing about a dead seed worth knowing.

Three decisions in it. The check lives in `TryAutoReseedOnExtinction()`, called from **both** paths that apply a step result — `ApplyStepResult()` (Play) and the completion lambda in `Next()` (manual **F**) — because extinction is equally interesting in both, and one shared helper is what keeps them from drifting apart. It is checked **per applied generation rather than on a timer in `Tick()`**: an empty grid is the *result* of a step, and with `StepsPerRender > 1` the dying generation may never reach the screen at all. And it sits **before** the counters and the render in both callers, since `NewSeed()` rebuilds the grid from scratch and resets them anyway (`RebuildGridFromCells()`) — so the empty frame is never drawn, and the switch between seeds is seamless.

Switching the mode on while the grid is *already* empty re-rolls immediately instead of waiting: there will be no further steps to notice extinction on — a dead grid stays dead — so the wait would be forever. For the same reason switching it on **starts the run** if it is stopped (`Start()`): nothing goes extinct while nothing is being computed, so "turn the search on" and "start searching" are one action, not two — otherwise Shift+N in its most typical moment (walked up to a dead grid, switched the mode on) would visibly do nothing at all. A held Shift+F auto-step counts as running and is left alone — it computes generations too, so the search is already under way, and `Start()` refuses during it anyway. Switching the mode **off** does *not* stop the run: "stop searching" is not "stop simulating". `Grid->Num()` is the maintained live-cell counter, not a scan, so the check costs nothing per generation; under a Generations rule it counts decaying cells too, which is correct here, since a grid holding only decaying cells is doomed anyway (a dying cell cannot be counted for birth).

**`ParityFilter` (`ECellParityFilter`, its own header per the `ESeedSymmetry` convention) — a face-centred cubic lattice for free, out of the cubic one.** Keep only the cells whose `x+y+z` is even and the result is *exactly* the FCC lattice (`{(x,y,z) ∈ Z³ : x+y+z even}`), whose 12 nearest neighbours are *exactly* the `d²=2` offsets, i.e. `ENeighborhood::Edges`. Every one of those 12 changes the coordinate sum by 0 or ±2, so parity is invariant: seed even, run `Edges`, and the automaton can never leave the sublattice. Under the ordinary `GridToWorld()` all 12 land at the same distance `√2·CellSize` — this is a genuine FCC automaton, not an imitation, and neither storage nor either compute strategy needed a single line, since both address cells by integer coordinates and never see the geometry at all. Three of the 14 neighbourhoods preserve parity and two are meaningful as FCC: `Edges` (12, first coordination shell) and `EdgesFarAxes` (18, first plus second). `Corners` *flips* parity every step, so the structure alternates between the two sublattices rather than living on one; every other neighbourhood leaves the sublattice within one generation, which makes the filter act on the initial state only and then dissolve.

Two decisions worth keeping straight. It filters inside **`FCellEmitter::Emit()`** — the single funnel all 13 generators already go through — rather than post-hoc over the emitted array, so rejects never occupy memory and the `MaxGeneratedCells` ceiling counts real cells; the trap there is that a parity rejection must return **`true`**, since `false` is the overflow signal every generator treats as "stop entirely" (`if (!Emit(...)) break;`), and returning it on an ordinary filtered cell would truncate the result at the first node of the wrong parity. And `EstimateCellCount()` is deliberately **not** adjusted: it is documented as a strict upper bound, a subset cannot violate one, and halving it would be *unsafe* — a generator may legitimately emit an all-even set (`LatticeBlocks` with an even `Period`), so `N/2` is not an upper bound. The estimate merely becomes twice as slack. `Odd` exists as its own value not for variety but because generators build **relative to the origin** (`Extent` is a half-size with the centre cell exactly at zero), so the centre's parity is fixed: a symmetric seed contains its centre cell under `Even` and has a hole there under `Odd`.

**`SameParity` is the same trick for BCC**, and the condition is genuinely different: `Even`/`Odd` test the parity of the *sum*, this one compares the three coordinates' parities *to each other*. The sets are not nested — `(1,1,0)` has an even sum but not matching parities. Cells with all-even coordinates form a cubic lattice of pitch 2 and all-odd cells sit exactly at its cube centres, which is the definition of body-centred cubic. Its 8 nearest neighbours are the cube diagonals at `√3` — `ENeighborhood::Corners` — and the next 6 are the axes at distance 2 — `FarAxes`; both are closed on the sublattice (a diagonal flips all three parities at once, staying inside the set; a far axis changes one coordinate by 2 and touches no parity at all), so `Corners` (8) and `CornersFarAxes` (14) are the meaningful neighbourhoods and both already existed.

**The Voronoi cell has exactly one face per nearest neighbour, and that rule ties the whole design together.** Cube: 6 faces ↔ 6 face-neighbours. Rhombic dodecahedron: 12 ↔ FCC's 12. BCC's cell is the **truncated octahedron** — 8 hexagonal faces looking down the 8 diagonals plus 6 square ones down the axes, 14 against `CornersFarAxes`'s 14. So the mesh is not an aesthetic choice: pick the neighbourhood and the only gap-free cell shape follows. Verified on the actual asset in Blender: 24 verts / 36 edges / 14 faces, exactly `{6: 8, 4: 6}` by side count, **volume 4.000000** (matching BCC's one point per volume 4), AABB `[-1,1]` on all three axes — a cube, so `CellMeshScaleMultiplier = 2.0` again, same as the rhombic dodecahedron.

Worth knowing where this ends: exactly **five** convex polyhedra tile space by translation (Fedorov, 1885) — cube, hexagonal prism, rhombic dodecahedron, elongated dodecahedron, truncated octahedron, with 6/8/12/12/14 faces and therefore that many neighbours. **Four of the five now work** — cube, RD, truncated octahedron, and the elongated dodecahedron, which needed exactly the different vertical pitch this paragraph once said it lacked (`LatticeZScale`, see `docs/lattice-and-cell-shapes.md`). Only the hexagonal prism is left, and it needs a sheared `GridToWorld` that `FLatticeTransform` cannot yet express. There is no sixth — it is a closed classification, so this is the end of the road rather than a pause in it.

`Generation.ParityFilter` covers BCC with the same shape of assertion as FCC and the same anti-vacuity guard: `Corners` and `CornersFarAxes` must keep every cell on the sublattice, `Moore` must break it. Measured: 0 strays of 1165 and of 1027 respectively, against 1122 of 2287 for `Moore`.

Since every entry point now goes through `GenerateState()` (see the paragraph above), FCC and BCC apply everywhere a state is built — **Y**, session start, **N**, and `Play` on an empty scene alike. **R** is the exception by nature rather than by omission: it replays `InitialStateCells` verbatim, so it reproduces whatever lattice that pattern was built on.

**Overload guard**: `GenerateState()` checks `StateGenerators::EstimateCellCount()` against `MaxGeneratedCells` (24M) **before touching the grid at all**, so a refusal leaves the current state intact — the bake-budget idiom, but deliberately **without** auto-coarsening (coarsening a lattice changes the geometry, and the geometry is the whole point). The limit isn't a low ceiling — the project's working scale is millions of live cells and this sits ~3× above it; it exists to catch the cases where one slider drag multiplies the volume by orders of magnitude (a solid box at `Extent=200` is 64M). Noise generators' estimates are *expected*, not upper bounds, so `FCellEmitter` re-checks the ceiling by fact as it goes. **Lattice-point counts for spheres are computed exactly**, in O(R²), not from `4/3πR³`: the continuous volume is not an upper bound on lattice points, and on a hollow sphere the two approximations' errors compound until the estimate falls *below* the truth and stops guarding anything (caught by `Generation.Determinism`, 708 vs 744). Because that's O(R²), the HUD's copy of the estimate refreshes on a 0.25 s timer rather than every tick.

Two traps worth not re-discovering: **negative modulo** (`-3 % 8` is `-3` in C++, so lattices need `WrapMod`/floor-division — the symptom is a pattern that looks right in the positive octant and tears exactly at zero, i.e. dead centre of the frame), and **`FMath::PerlinNoise3D` returning exactly 0 at integer points** — with a "round" `NoiseScale` (1.0/0.5/0.25) the field degenerates and the result comes out empty or solid, hence the default `0.08` and the warning in `SetStateGeneratorParams()`. Perlin takes no seed at all, so "different seeds" are different sampling offsets (±512, with a fractional part).
